## Course outline

The original Intro plan deliberately limits technical detail. Its first purpose is to explain what we will make. For new club members, knowing what others enjoy, who can help, and how to ask questions is part of being ready to create.

My introduction connects my computing background, game-development studies at SMU and personal engine work, without making that experience a prerequisite. Introductions from teaching assistants help communicate that participants can join the discussion before they know how to build a game.

## Learning goals

The goals are to understand the course and jam, locate a saved project and choose a small game to observe.

The plan includes voluntary introductions and recording recommended PICO-8 games. With a larger group, it does not require everyone to speak: volunteers can establish the pattern while others retain space to ask questions.

## A minimal tool workflow

The introduction covers only what the next lessons immediately need: code, sprite, map and sound editors, followed by loading, saving and running a cartridge. APIs can be introduced when a concrete task calls for them.

| Action | Example | What to check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Save | `save first_game.p8` | Know where the file is stored |
| Load | `load first_game.p8` | Reopen the project just saved |
| Run | `run` | Observe the result after editing |
| Open directory | `folder` | Distinguish the cartridge from a demonstration video |

A short save-and-reload cycle helps prevent file-location problems during later live coding. See the [official PICO-8 manual](https://www.lexaloffle.com/dl/docs/pico-8_manual.html) for operational details.

## Turning play into a making question

The original closing activity asks members to choose a PICO-8 game and play together. The edited observation exercise asks three things: what does the player repeatedly do, what feedback answers that action, and which single rule might be changed to produce a different experience?

The purpose is to translate “fun” into observable behavior rather than immediately judge the work. Subsequent lessons can then ask what input, state and presentation would support that experience.

## Next lessons

Later sessions combine explanations, follow-along work and exercises before the jam. The plan includes platform movement, dashing, sound and levels, with adjustments as the course progresses.
