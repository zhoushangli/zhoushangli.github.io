## Context

I was the main instructor for four PICO-8 development sessions for new members of our game club: an introduction and three main classes. Each lasted approximately 90 minutes with around ten or slightly more attendees. Teaching combined explanation and live coding that participants could follow, with Q&A and exercise support outside class. A week-long club game jam followed the course.

The sessions total approximately six teaching hours. Attendance is an approximate per-session figure, not a count of unique students. Teaching assistants and other contributors supported the series; I do not attribute all sound or level-design instruction to myself.

## Learning goals

The aim was to help participants complete a small, editable game and explain their choices. PICO-8 brings code, graphics, maps and sound into a compact environment, making it useful for connecting a change in code to a visible result.

I organized the progression around three questions: how does input produce a visible response, how do we organize a growing program, and how does that technical work serve a manageable game? Understanding why the next change is needed matters more than memorizing every API at once.

## From following to modifying

During live coding I introduced a small goal, demonstrated the change, let participants follow, and paused between stages for questions. Even a simple example should leave room for independent work: after demonstrating horizontal movement, ask learners to add vertical movement and explain whether they changed input, position or rendering.

Exercise sessions provided further practice. When debugging, the useful questions were what the learner expected, what happened, and where the two first diverged. This turns support into a way of understanding a program rather than simply receiving corrected code.

## Game jam and submissions

The jam shifted the task from following a lesson to making a game. The retained L03 plan emphasizes a runnable prototype, an explanation of design choices, and revisions informed by playtesting. Interim feedback, an Alpha playtest and a showcase are documented as planned stages; the plan alone does not prove each stage happened exactly as written.

## Record and reflection

The supplied [Pico8 Training L03 recording link](https://meeting.tencent.com/crm/NARXvk9acd) is dated October 7, 2026. It is an external Chinese-language resource. This page is not a timestamped transcript or a review of the recording.

The available materials show a curriculum, exercises and submitted work. They do not include systematic pre/post assessments or complete feedback statistics, so they cannot establish a uniform level of mastery. Future improvements would include checking programming prerequisites earlier, reducing the amount of code changed in one demonstration, and retaining evidence of how students explain and revise their own work.
